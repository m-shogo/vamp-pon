# All Character Garment Comfort / Pressure / Chafing / Thermal / Long-Wear Fidelity Master v1

Status: `CURRENT_PRODUCTION_VISUAL_AUTHORITY`

Scope: all 36 characters, all 9 production asset kinds.

## Purpose

This Master prevents a garment from being visually correct, mechanically constructible and technically wearable while still behaving like a costume that no living character could plausibly inhabit for ordinary time.

The governing chain is:

**Authorized Body / Mobility → Garment Construction → Material Mechanics → Fit / Layering → Contact / Pressure / Friction → Heat / Moisture / Motion → Long-Wear Response → Adjustment / Maintenance**

This authority governs physical plausibility. It does **not** invent medical conditions, sensory sensitivities, pain tolerance, disability accommodations, modesty preferences, fashion preferences or personality traits.

## Core rule

**Long-wear behavior follows the authorized body, garment and use context; it does not redesign them.**

A collar, waistband, cuff, strap, seam, shoe, seat-contact panel or layered area must create pressure, friction, heat and moisture behavior consistent with its construction and use.

The image model may not beautify away load-bearing contact, nor may it exaggerate discomfort into character drama.

## Unknown-comfort rule

Unknown long-wear behavior defaults to:

`SOURCE_CONSTRAINED_NEUTRAL_LONG_WEAR_COMFORT_COMPLETION`

This means:

1. preserve authored body, mobility, garment, material, fit and dressing workflow;
2. infer only ordinary physically necessary contact, ventilation and pressure consequences;
3. assume neither exceptional comfort nor exceptional discomfort;
4. do not infer medical, sensory, psychological, trauma, cultural, gender or sexuality traits from clothing contact;
5. do not add comfort devices, padding, liners, bandages, compression wear, orthopedic support, anti-chafe panels or cooling systems without authority;
6. generated long-wear behavior remains `CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and never creates Canon.

## Pressure rule

Pressure requires a source and a load path.

Valid causes include:

- elastic or authored stretch;
- waistbands and cuffs;
- closures under load;
- straps and harnesses;
- body weight against a seat/backrest;
- footwear containment;
- layered bulk;
- stored-object weight;
- repeated posture.

Invalid shortcuts include:

- drawing arbitrary red pressure marks for drama;
- adding compression merely to make the body look slimmer;
- removing all pressure cues from load-bearing straps for beauty;
- inventing padding because a contact area looks uncomfortable;
- shrinking body mass so a strap or waistband appears looser.

## Chafing / friction rule

Chafing risk is a mechanical possibility, not a guaranteed injury and not a personality trait.

It depends on:

- contact pressure;
- material friction;
- repeated movement;
- moisture;
- seam/hardware placement;
- duration.

Do not automatically render irritation, wounds, scars, bandages or protective products. Those require separate authority.

## Thermal rule

Thermal behavior follows layer count, material bulk, coverage, ventilation and environment.

- multiple insulating layers retain more heat than one thin layer;
- open construction may ventilate only where openings actually exist;
- wind changes convective behavior but does not create new vents;
- wet material may alter heat transfer according to material authority;
- dark/light color alone does not authorize exaggerated thermal storytelling;
- night scenes do not automatically require more layers;
- seasonal art may not replace the baseline garment without authorized variant delta.

The model may not infer that a character is cold-natured, heat-sensitive or unusually tolerant.

## Moisture rule

Moisture is material and environment behavior.

It may alter:

- local friction;
- weight;
- drying rate;
- fold response;
- thermal response;
- local cling within existing material authority.

It may not:

- create sexualized transparency;
- invent sweat patterns as personality or attractiveness coding;
- create stains as Canon;
- reveal unsupported anatomy;
- justify removing layers or opening closures unless scene authority requires it.

## Seated / wheelchair rule

Seated use is not a temporary exception to the design.

Preserve:

- finite cloth and padding thickness;
- seat/back compression;
- waistband and torso pressure redistribution;
- thigh/hip contact;
- pocket-object placement relative to seat pressure;
- seam/hardware placement relative to repeated contact;
- wheelchair frame and cushion clearance;
- reachable closures and adjustment points where already authorized.

Do not slim the body, flatten layers, move pockets, erase hardware or invent cushioning to make the seated silhouette cleaner.

## Prosthetic / orthotic / assistive-equipment boundary

Garment contact with prosthetic, orthotic or assistive equipment remains source-constrained.

The model may preserve mechanically necessary clearance and friction behavior, but may not infer:

- pain;
- skin injury;
- medical diagnosis;
- preferred liner system;
- compression garments;
- padding;
- helper routines;
- device-removal habits.

Those require explicit authority.

## Footwear rule

Footwear participates in long-wear body mechanics.

Preserve:

- authorized foot scale;
- toe-box and instep volume;
- heel containment;
- sole stiffness/flex relation;
- closure pressure;
- sock/stocking layer thickness;
- walking bend zones.

Do not:

- shrink feet for elegant shoes;
- add heels for leg length;
- invent cushioning, insoles, elastic, side zippers or orthopedic support;
- render impossible zero-pressure tight boots;
- add blisters, wounds or bandages without authority.

## Hardware rule

Buttons, snaps, buckles, zippers, hooks and fasteners have thickness and contact consequences.

Hardware may not float above the body to avoid contact. It also may not be buried impossibly into soft tissue.

Contact must remain compatible with:

- garment layers;
- facing/lining;
- closure load;
- seated posture;
- neighboring equipment.

## Adjustment rule

Ordinary readjustment is physical behavior, not Canon personality.

A garment may mechanically require occasional:

- strap settling;
- waistband settling;
- cuff/hem alignment;
- closure re-seating;
- pocket-load redistribution;
- collar/hood settling.

Do not turn this into a habitual gesture, nervous tic, flirtation cue, disability cue or character-specific ritual unless source-backed.

## Long-wear temporal continuity

Time changes cloth through existing material mechanics.

Possible neutral changes include:

- folds accumulating at repeated flexion zones;
- straps settling into stable load positions;
- temporary compression of padding;
- moisture accumulation/drying;
- ordinary wrinkle development.

These are candidate-state consequences, not permanent Canon wear marks.

Do not reset every asset to factory-fresh cloth, but do not turn one generated crease or pressure mark into a signature feature.

## Maintenance boundary

Long-wear consequences may reveal whether existing construction is easy or difficult to access for ordinary maintenance, but they do not authorize new maintenance habits.

Generated comfort problems may not create:

- patches;
- replacement closures;
- new liners;
- altered seams;
- new straps;
- cutouts;
- permanent stretch;
- personalized modifications.

Maintenance changes require their existing authority and human review.

## Premium rule

Premium art does not make clothing less livable or more sexualized.

Prohibited premium defaults include:

- tighter waistbands;
- tighter chest/hip fit;
- sharper strap indentation;
- glossy wet skin near pressure zones;
- high heels;
- thinner footwear;
- more exposed ventilation;
- opened collars/closures;
- bare-skin cutouts;
- decorative compression straps;
- corsetry;
- unrealistic zero-bulk layering;
- erasure of wheelchair/seat contact.

Premium may improve readability only.

## LOD / chibi / sprite rule

Small-scale assets may omit minor contact wrinkles and tiny pressure cues.

Preserve before micro-detail:

- major load-bearing straps/belts;
- layer bulk;
- footwear volume;
- seat/wheelchair contact;
- major closure/hardware placement;
- body/garment clearance;
- major ventilation/opening structure.

Do not simplify long-wear mechanics by shrinking the body, equipment or footwear.

## Production review checklist

Before accepting a candidate, verify:

- pressure has a real closure/strap/seat/layer/footwear cause;
- body mass is not reduced to solve contact;
- friction and moisture follow material and motion rather than drama;
- no injury, diagnosis, sensitivity or accommodation was invented;
- thermal behavior matches coverage/layering/environment without personality inference;
- seated and wheelchair contact preserve finite layer/body/equipment geometry;
- footwear volume and load remain plausible;
- premium did not add compression, heels, exposure or zero-bulk tailoring;
- LOD removed micro cues before major load/contact structures;
- unsupported long-wear behavior remains `CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

## Canon boundary

Generated pressure, friction, chafing risk, thermal response, moisture response, sweat placement, comfort adjustment, pain implication, wear habit or maintenance adaptation never creates Canon by repetition or plausibility.

Human-reviewed authority is required for promotion.
