# All Character Detail Density / Ornament Budget Fidelity Master v1

Status: CURRENT_PRODUCTION_VISUAL_AUTHORITY  
Scope: all 36 characters / all 9 production asset kinds  
Output status: CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Purpose
Prevent premium rendering, high resolution, rarity signaling, importance signaling, fantasy shorthand, state transforms, event costumes, cut-ins, and asset escalation from adding unsupported ornament or collapsing all characters into one gacha-style information density.

## Core principle
Information density is not value, importance, rarity, beauty, or professionalism.

A visual detail is allowed only when it has an authorized job: identity, construction, use, storage, repair, material transition, movement, era/world translation, or a source-backed relationship/history trace. Ornament without a job is removed before anything new is added.

## Detail priority
1. identity-defining face/body/posture information
2. garment construction and closure logic
3. exposure/body-modification boundaries
4. hand/contact and prop-use relationships
5. mobility equipment and accessibility-critical structure
6. material transitions and maintenance history
7. required era/world construction cues
8. required relationship/history traces
9. secondary seams/folds/fasteners
10. decorative detail

## Ornament budget rules
- `OPEN` never grants decorative freedom.
- premium/high-resolution assets may describe authorized structure more clearly but may not increase ornament count automatically.
- protagonist, boss, rare, limited, event, or story-important status may not increase gold trim, gems, belts, harnesses, floating cloth, glow nodes, badges, tassels, embroidery, exposed skin, or dangling hardware automatically.
- a decorative element must have a local reason and a removal cost. If removing it changes nothing important, it is not identity-critical.
- repeated micro-ornament should be collapsed into a few intentional anchors.
- world motifs are expressed through construction, seams, folds, fasteners, material choice, use, wear, storage, and light behavior before icons or emblems.
- star/paper/ink/light motifs may not be pasted onto every surface.
- relationship closeness does not create matching accessories, pair jewelry, paired tattoos, synchronized trim, or shared emblems without source authority.
- state transforms may change authorized condition, not decoration density.
- battle states may not gain random straps/spikes/shards.
- calm states may not gain ribbons/flowers/soft ornament by default.
- Dawn may not gain automatic white/gold filigree.
- Kokuyou may not gain automatic black/neon hardware.
- small-scale assets remove low-value detail before adding readability decorations.
- generated ornament accidents never create canon.

## Per-category budget
### Metal
Allowed: closures, tool interfaces, structural fasteners, mobility hardware, source-backed jewelry.  
Not allowed by default: gold trim, decorative chains, floating metal ornaments, gem settings, ornamental buckles with no function.

### Cloth
Allowed: construction seams, hems, reinforcement, repair, fit/mobility solutions, source-backed patterning.  
Not allowed by default: extra ribbons, detached sleeves, floating scarf tails, decorative cape layers, unexplained cutouts.

### Leather / straps
Allowed: load-bearing straps, storage, footwear, tool retention, structural reinforcement.  
Not allowed by default: belt multiplication, chest harness filler, thigh straps without carried function.

### Gems / emissive nodes
Default budget: zero unless authority requires them. They may not be used as generic premium signals or readability crutches.

### Embroidery / symbols
Allowed only with source-backed personal, institutional, cultural, functional, or world-construction reason. Do not distribute symbols evenly just to make a costume feel finished.

### Skin exposure
Never counts as an ornament budget solution. Exposure follows Living Visual preference and context, not visual-density balancing.

## Information density invariants
1. detail density may vary by character without implying status hierarchy.
2. premium assets may increase descriptive clarity, not decorative count.
3. high resolution may reveal authorized construction, not invent micro-decoration.
4. character importance may not add ornament.
5. rarity/event framing may not add ornament.
6. face/body identity must not compete with decorative noise.
7. main prop must remain more semantically important than secondary trinkets.
8. mobility equipment must not be visually demoted by decorative clutter.
9. tools remain tools, not costume jewelry.
10. repair traces remain functional history, not aesthetic distressed texture spam.
11. material transitions remain legible beneath ornament.
12. garment openings/closures remain readable beneath ornament.
13. no accessory may silently alter piercing/tattoo/body-modification policy.
14. no trim/cutout may silently alter exposure policy.
15. no ornament may be added to compensate for weak silhouette.
16. no ornament may be added to compensate for same-face collision.
17. no ornament may be added to compensate for weak color separation.
18. no ornament may be added to compensate for weak value hierarchy.
19. no ornament may be added to compensate for weak line/edge readability.
20. no ornament may be added to compensate for weak prop relation.
21. no ornament may be added to compensate for generic clothing construction.
22. no world motif may be repeated solely for brand recognition.
23. decorative symmetry/asymmetry must be character- and construction-led, not generic polish.
24. generated detail treatment remains candidate-only.

## Unknown default
Unknown detail density defaults to `MINIMUM_SUFFICIENT_AUTHORIZED_DETAIL`.

When uncertain:
- keep existing identity anchors;
- keep required construction and function;
- omit decorative additions;
- use neutral closures/fasteners only when physically necessary;
- do not invent gems, jewelry, embroidery, harnesses, cutouts, emblems, trim, glow nodes, decorative weapons, or floating cloth.

## Review test
For each visible detail ask:
1. What authorized job does it perform?
2. Which authority supports it?
3. If removed, what identity/function/history becomes worse?
4. Does it conflict with personal taste, exposure, piercing/tattoo, maintenance, era, relationship, or mobility rules?
5. Is it being used to hide a deeper design weakness?

If the answer is unclear, remove it or keep it candidate-only.

## Promotion rule
Generated details never become canon because they look good, recur across outputs, or make an image feel more finished. Promotion requires explicit human review and source/provenance recording.
