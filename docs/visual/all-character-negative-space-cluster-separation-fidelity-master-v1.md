## Purpose
Preserve readable character identity by treating negative space, overlap gaps, cluster separation, silhouette breathing room, and contact-zone clarity as production design information rather than unused area to be filled.

## Core principle
Empty space is not missing detail. It is a readability structure.

Hair, limbs, garments, props, mobility equipment, tools, effects, and environmental elements may overlap only when the overlap communicates authorized action or construction. Premium polish, action posing, rarity, or cinematic framing may not collapse all clusters into one tangled silhouette.

## Separation priority
1. face from hair/effects/foreground clutter
2. head/neck from shoulder and collar masses
3. arms/hands from torso when contact intent matters
4. hands from props/tools at grip/contact zones
5. legs from skirt/coat/cloth masses where body category or stance matters
6. mobility equipment from body, garment, ground, and environmental clutter
7. main prop from secondary props/effects
8. garment layers from each other at construction-critical openings
9. animal limbs/tail/ears from body silhouette when identity-bearing
10. robot shell modules/interfaces from surrounding clutter

## Negative-space invariants
1. face-reading space may not be consumed by decorative foreground effects.
2. eye/brow/mouth reading may not depend on removing authorized hair shape.
3. neck/shoulder relation must remain readable enough to preserve body category.
4. hand/contact zones require local separation when contact is identity- or action-critical.
5. main prop grip must remain distinguishable from fingers, sleeves, straps, and effects.
6. mobility equipment must retain readable structural gaps and body relation.
7. wheelchair wheels/frame may not merge into decorative background geometry.
8. garment layers must retain enough separation to understand actual construction.
9. coat/cape/hem motion may not erase leg stance merely for dynamism.
10. hair masses may not merge with shoulder garments into a new silhouette by accident.
11. long cloth may not cross every limb to manufacture motion.
12. secondary accessories may not occupy identity-critical breathing room.
13. effects may not fill silhouette gaps merely to make a frame feel richer.
14. environmental foreground objects may not hide identity-critical structure without scene authority.
15. premium assets may not reduce negative space automatically.
16. high-resolution assets may not fill open regions with decorative particles.
17. rarity/importance may not increase cluster density automatically.
18. small-scale assets simplify clusters before adding separation outlines or symbols.
19. chibi abstraction must preserve relative cluster distinctions across characters.
20. portrait crop must not force hair/prop/effect tangles around the face.
21. action pose may overlap more, but every major overlap needs a readable action reason.
22. calm pose may not be artificially spread only to create graphic spacing.
23. relationship scenes may not force paired silhouettes or touching clusters without source authority.
24. body exposure may not be increased to create negative-space contrast.
25. garment cutouts may not be invented to create separation.
26. props may not be detached unnaturally from the body merely to create icon-like readability.
27. tools and storage must preserve believable reach/use even when separated visually.
28. animal tails/ears/fur masses may not be repositioned into mascot-like spacing by default.
29. robot modules may not be exploded apart visually unless functionally articulated.
30. generated overlap/spacing accidents never create canon.

## Unknown default
Unknown spacing defaults to `FUNCTIONAL_IDENTITY_PRESERVING_BREATHING_ROOM`.

When no exact pose/layout authority exists:
- keep face unobstructed enough to read established geometry;
- preserve body-category and garment-construction gaps;
- preserve hand/prop and mobility-equipment relations;
- keep effects outside identity-critical gaps;
- prefer fewer, clearer clusters over tangled richness;
- do not invent spread poses, cutouts, detached cloth, floating props, white outlines, or extra accessories to manufacture separation.

## Cluster budget
A cluster is a visually connected mass such as head/hair, torso/upper garment, lower garment, arm/hand, main prop, mobility device, or major effect mass.

- More clusters do not mean better design.
- Fewer clusters do not mean simpler personality.
- A cluster must correspond to authorized anatomy, construction, object use, mobility, or effect logic.
- When clusters collide, reduce decorative/effect density first.
- Do not split one garment into fake detached components merely to improve graphic rhythm.
- Do not merge separate garments merely to simplify silhouette.

## Asset-kind guidance
### Character reference
Prefer maximum auditability. Keep face, body category, garment layers, hands/props, footwear and mobility structure legible.

### Portrait / cut-in
Face and upper-body breathing room outrank decorative framing. Do not ring the face with particles, hair spikes, ribbons, weapons, or glow merely to fill crop space.

### Full illustration
Foreground/environment overlap is allowed when scene-grounded, but identity-critical clusters must remain recoverable.

### Sprite / chibi
Use a few separated masses and value groups. Do not solve crowding with white outlines, enlarged eyes, shortened mobility equipment, exposure, or new accessories.

### Battle/state assets
Motion can compress gaps transiently, but canonical body/garment/object relationships must remain inferable.

## Forbidden shortcuts
- premium fills empty space
- particles in every silhouette gap
- hair framing every face edge
- cape crossing both legs
- cloth crossing every limb
- weapon behind head for composition by default
- prop floating away from grip for readability
- white outline to separate tangled clusters
- glow outline to separate tangled clusters
- exposure to create body/garment separation
- thigh cutout to separate leg silhouette
- abdomen cutout to separate torso layers
- detached sleeve to create negative space
- extra straps to divide large cloth masses
- extra belts to create visual segmentation
- floating scarf tails to fill corners
- foreground flowers/paper/shards over face
- decorative smoke around hands
- effects hiding finger/prop contact
- hair hiding weak jaw mismatch
- collar hiding weak neck/body mismatch
- skirt mass hiding leg/body-category mismatch
- darkness hiding mobility equipment overlap
- background geometry swallowing wheelchair frame
- wheel rim treated as ornamental halo
- animal tail repositioned as mascot icon
- robot modules separated as decorative floating parts
- relationship pair silhouettes forced together
- rarity increasing overlap density
- high-resolution increasing particle density
- generated spacing accident promoted to canon

## Review test
Ask:
1. Can the face be read without changing authorized hair?
2. Can body category and posture be read from the remaining gaps?
3. Are garment layers and closures understandable?
4. Are hand/prop/tool contacts readable?
5. Is mobility equipment structurally readable and integrated?
6. Does every major overlap have an action, construction, or scene reason?
7. Could decorative/effect clutter be removed before changing anatomy or clothing?
