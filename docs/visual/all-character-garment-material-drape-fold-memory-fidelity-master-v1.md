# All Character Garment Material / Drape / Fold Memory Fidelity Master v1

Status: `CURRENT_PRODUCTION_VISUAL_AUTHORITY`

Scope: all 36 characters, all 9 production asset kinds.

## Purpose

This Master prevents every garment from collapsing into the same thin, glossy, weightless AI cloth after body, fit and pattern topology have already been resolved correctly.

The governing chain is:

**Garment Construction → Material Class → Thickness / Stiffness / Stretch / Friction → Gravity / Contact / Motion / Moisture → Drape / Fold / Crease / Recovery → Visible Surface**

Surface rendering is the final expression of material mechanics, not permission to replace them.

## Core rule

**Material mechanics must remain coherent with the authored garment construction and use.**

A coat, knit, shirt, lining, leather-like panel, rigid shell, soft wrap, sock, shoe upper, strap or padded section may not share one universal fold language merely because the image style is anime or premium.

Front, 3/4, profile, rear, seated, crouched, moving, wet, wind-exposed, premium, chibi and sprite outputs must preserve the same material-mechanics family unless an authorized state actually changes the material condition.

## Unknown-material rule

Unknown garment mechanics default to:

`SOURCE_CONSTRAINED_NEUTRAL_MATERIAL_MECHANICS_COMPLETION`

This means:

1. preserve source-backed material class, construction, thickness cues, wear and weather behavior;
2. infer only the minimum ordinary mechanics needed to make the existing garment physically coherent;
3. prefer neutral moderate behavior over silk-like cling, leather-like stiffness, rubber-like stretch, metallic shine or weightless chiffon motion;
4. do not infer luxury, rarity, sexuality, wealth, combat role, gender presentation or character personality from material behavior;
5. generated material mechanics remain `CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and never create canon.

## Thickness rule

Thickness is structural information.

- thick layers create visible edge mass and larger fold radii;
- thin layers may form smaller folds but do not automatically cling to anatomy;
- multiple layers must accumulate believable bulk;
- cuffs, hems, facings, waistbands, collars and reinforced edges may be thicker than a single body panel when construction supports it;
- seam intersections cannot become infinitely thin merely for clean rendering;
- padding may compress locally but does not disappear.

Do not use zero-thickness cloth as a universal solution to clipping.

## Stiffness rule

Bending stiffness controls silhouette stability and fold scale.

Stiffer material tends toward:

- broader, fewer bends;
- larger fold radii;
- stronger panel memory;
- more stable collars, cuffs, plackets or shells;
- less body tracing without explicit shaping.

Softer material tends toward:

- more gravity-following drape;
- smaller secondary folds where load supports them;
- more contact response;
- less self-supporting silhouette.

Do not turn all clothing into either rigid cardboard or fluid silk.

## Fold-causality rule

Every major fold must have a cause:

- gravity;
- a seam or closure constraint;
- body contact;
- joint flexion;
- compression;
- material excess/ease;
- wind;
- carried load;
- previous crease memory.

Decorative zig-zag fold noise is not construction.

## Fold-memory rule

Material has temporal continuity.

A garment may retain:

- elbow or knee creases;
- repeated seated folds;
- storage folds;
- pleat memory;
- compressed padding state;
- softened wear zones;

only when compatible with material and maintenance authority.

A new camera angle does not reset the garment to factory-flat. Premium art does not erase ordinary crease history for beauty. Conversely, image-generation accidents do not become permanent signature wrinkles.

## Recovery rule

After motion or compression, cloth returns according to material mechanics.

- elastic/knit structures may recover faster;
- heavy or creased materials may recover slowly;
- rigid panels may retain shape;
- wet material may recover differently from dry material;
- padding may rebound rather than remain crushed forever.

Do not snap every garment instantly back to a perfect neutral silhouette between frames.

## Wetness rule

Weather authority remains stronger.

Wetness may change:

- mass;
- stiffness;
- local cling;
- fold amplitude;
- surface value/specularity;
- recovery time.

Wetness may not:

- make opaque cloth suddenly transparent without source/material authority;
- reveal unsupported anatomy;
- sexualize body contours;
- erase layer thickness;
- turn every material glossy;
- create permanent canonical stains or deformation.

## Wind rule

Wind response follows mass, area, stiffness and attachment.

- heavy panels react later and less dramatically;
- light loose edges may flutter more;
- attached hems and closures constrain motion;
- straps, bags and hoods retain anchor logic;
- a coat cannot become weightless floating ribbons for premium composition.

Wind does not redesign the garment.

## Contact and seated rule

Seat, backrest, body and equipment contact must affect cloth through material mechanics.

- soft layers compress;
- thick layers retain bulk;
- stiff seams may resist flattening;
- folds accumulate around hip, thigh, knee and torso flexion according to existing fit;
- wheelchair contact and assistive equipment clearance remain visible and functional;
- cloth does not float over seats or pass through equipment.

## Layering rule

Layer stacks preserve thickness and friction.

- underlayers may change outer drape subtly;
- outerwear cannot lie perfectly flush over bulky layers unless construction allows it;
- collars/hoods/waistbands stack with finite thickness;
- friction may prevent unlimited sliding;
- hidden layers may not be deleted to obtain a cleaner silhouette.

## Pocket and load rule

Stored objects and pocket contents influence material locally.

- pocket bags may sag or project according to load;
- heavy objects create localized tension through real anchors;
- thin material cannot support implausible weight without deformation/reinforcement;
- object scale must remain compatible with pocket volume;
- pocket load is not permission to alter body anatomy.

## Material-class differentiation

Do not collapse distinct classes into one render language.

Examples of mechanically different behavior may include:

- light woven cloth;
- heavy woven cloth;
- knit/rib material;
- padded/quilted material;
- leather-like or coated material where authorized;
- rigid protective panel where authorized;
- lining;
- strap/webbing;
- sock/stocking material;
- footwear upper;
- fur/shell/nonhuman covering when garment logic intersects it.

Unknown exact composition stays unknown. Mechanical completion must remain conservative.

## Surface / lighting boundary

Highlights, shading and line work must describe the material mechanics already present.

They may not:

- fake thinness by removing edge mass;
- fake stiffness with arbitrary hard highlights;
- fake softness by blurring construction seams;
- fake body shape through specular bands;
- replace fold causality with glossy gradients;
- use rim light to hide layer intersections.

## Premium rule

Premium art may increase rendering clarity, not change material physics.

Prohibited premium defaults:

- silk-like cling;
- satin gloss;
- wet-look highlights;
- sharper body-tracing folds;
- extra wind lift;
- weightless floating hems;
- pristine wrinkle erasure;
- stiffer heroic coat tails;
- thinner fabric for elegance;
- transparency increase;
- decorative micro-fold density.

## LOD / chibi / sprite rule

Small-scale conversion removes material detail in this order:

1. micro wrinkles;
2. minor crease branches;
3. subtle texture;
4. tiny seam-bulk cues;
5. small friction/contact ripples.

Preserve before them:

- thickness family;
- stiff vs soft silhouette behavior;
- major fold direction;
- major crease memory;
- layer bulk;
- major contact compression;
- pocket/load deformation;
- garment topology and fit.

Small scale may not turn every garment into the same flat paper shape.

## Production review checklist

Before accepting a candidate, verify:

- thickness is finite and compatible with garment construction;
- fold radius and density match stiffness and material family;
- major folds have gravity/contact/motion/load causes;
- crease memory is temporally coherent rather than random;
- wetness and wind follow material mechanics without increasing exposure;
- layers retain bulk and friction;
- seat/wheelchair/equipment contact is physically coherent;
- pockets and straps deform under real loads;
- premium rendering did not change material class or cloth physics;
- LOD removed micro detail before major material identity;
- unsupported mechanics remain `CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

## Canon boundary

Generated thickness, stiffness, stretch, fold, crease, drape, wrinkle, wet response, wind response, friction, compression, recovery or material-class inference never creates Canon by repetition or visual plausibility.

Human-reviewed authority is required for promotion.
