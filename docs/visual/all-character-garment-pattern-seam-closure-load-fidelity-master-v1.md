# All Character Garment Pattern / Seam / Closure / Load Fidelity Master v1

Status: `CURRENT_PRODUCTION_VISUAL_AUTHORITY`

Scope: all 36 characters, all 9 production asset kinds.

## Purpose

This Master prevents image generation from drawing clothing as an attractive surface without a believable construction underneath it.

The previous Garment-to-Body Fit authority establishes how cloth fits, clears, tensions and compresses around the authorized body. This authority goes one level deeper and asks whether the garment itself can exist as a constructed object.

The governing chain is:

**Authorized Body → Garment Pattern Pieces → Seam / Dart / Pleat / Closure Topology → Load Paths → Material Response → Fit / Tension / Compression → Visible Silhouette / Folds**

Do not reverse this chain. A visually pleasing fold, neckline, slit, belt, buckle, zipper, opening, trim or panel boundary is not permission to invent the construction required to support it.

## Core construction rule

**Every visible garment feature must belong to one coherent pattern-and-assembly system.**

Front, 3/4, profile, rear, seated, crouched, reaching, moving, damaged, wet, premium, chibi and sprite views must describe the same garment topology unless an explicit authorized variant delta changes it.

A seam is not decoration. A dart is not a highlight. A zipper is not a graphic line. A button is not floating ornament. A strap is not a generic gacha detail. Each one implies construction, attachment, load and an opposite side or termination.

## Unknown-construction rule

Unknown garment construction defaults to:

`SOURCE_CONSTRAINED_MINIMUM_MECHANICALLY_VALID_GARMENT_COMPLETION`

This means:

1. preserve all source-backed garment panels, silhouette, closures, openings, layers, storage and fit;
2. continue only mechanically necessary hidden construction;
3. use the smallest ordinary solution compatible with known material and function;
4. do not invent decorative seams, darts, princess lines, corsetry, belts, buckles, zippers, lacing, straps, harnesses, pleats, slits, cutouts, vents, trim channels or ornamental paneling;
5. do not infer tailoring complexity from rarity, premium status, gender presentation, attractiveness, combat role or fantasy convention;
6. generated construction remains `CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and never creates canon.

## Pattern-piece continuity

Pattern pieces must remain the same objects across viewpoints.

- front panels do not become different rear panels merely because the camera moves;
- a side seam must arrive at plausible front/back pieces;
- sleeves must connect to the same armhole topology in every view;
- collars, hoods and lapels retain their attachment points;
- hems, vents and slits retain their location and count;
- pocket bags do not disappear when the opening is visible;
- lining and facing may be visually hidden, but visible edge behavior must remain compatible with their presence or absence.

Unknown rear or interior construction is not an empty design surface.

## Seam rule

A visible seam requires a construction reason.

Valid seam jobs include:

- joining authored pattern pieces;
- shaping volume when source-backed;
- attaching sleeves, collars, hoods, cuffs, yokes, waistbands or pockets;
- creating a material transition;
- carrying an authorized repair;
- supporting a closure or functional reinforcement.

Invalid seam use includes:

- adding random panel lines for detail density;
- drawing princess seams to make a body look slimmer;
- using V-shaped seams to manufacture a narrower waist;
- adding shoulder seams to exaggerate heroic breadth;
- adding under-bust or hip seams to sexualize anatomy;
- using seam highlights as fake musculature;
- inventing asymmetry simply because a rear or side view feels empty.

## Dart / shaping rule

Darts, tucks, gathers and shaped seams alter three-dimensional volume. Therefore they are body-sensitive construction decisions.

Do not add them merely to make a garment look fitted.

An unknown garment must not acquire:

- bust darts;
- waist darts;
- fisheye darts;
- princess seams;
- corset-like paneling;
- shaping tucks;
- body-accent gathers;

unless supported by existing construction authority.

## Closure rule

Every closure must answer four questions:

1. what opens;
2. where the opening begins and ends;
3. how the two sides meet;
4. what carries the load when closed.

Zippers require a route, termination and receiving edge. Buttons require buttonholes or a mechanically equivalent receiving structure. Buckles require strap continuation and anchor points. Lacing requires reinforced anchors and a closure gap. Drawcords require a channel and exits.

Do not use closures as floating decoration.

## Load-path rule

Visible tension must terminate in real construction.

Examples:

- bag weight travels through strap anchors into garment/body contact;
- a belt load travels around a waistband or garment body rather than pinching empty space;
- a zipper under tension transfers load into adjacent panels and seam reinforcement;
- a seated trouser load distributes through rise, seat, side/inseam and waistband construction;
- sleeve reach load transfers through sleeve cap, armhole, shoulder/back ease and material deformation;
- wheelchair or assistive-device contact may require clearance or reinforcement but does not authorize redesign.

No load path may be solved by inventing anatomy, shrinking the body or adding decorative harnesses.

## Closure and exposure boundary

Construction problems never grant exposure permission.

Prohibited shortcuts include:

- opening a zipper because the torso is difficult to fit;
- unbuttoning a shirt to solve chest tension;
- adding a slit to solve stride;
- raising a hem to solve crouching;
- widening a neckline to solve hair/collar overlap;
- rolling sleeves to avoid elbow construction;
- removing a layer to reduce bulk;
- adding a cutout to avoid seam or strap intersections.

Exposure remains governed by the stronger exposure authority.

## Storage / pocket construction

Pockets are physical volumes, not printed symbols.

When present, preserve:

- opening location;
- opening orientation;
- pocket bag or patch volume;
- attachment method;
- reinforcement at load points;
- relation to stored object scale;
- seated and motion clearance;
- continuity across viewpoints.

A pocket must not migrate merely to make the composition cleaner.

## Layering construction

Layers require construction clearance and compatible opening logic.

- outer closures must accommodate the authorized underlayer volume;
- collars and hoods must stack without impossible interpenetration;
- waistbands and belts must not occupy mutually impossible layers;
- straps must pass over/under layers according to known use;
- lining does not become a second decorative garment;
- premium art may not add extra underlayers, corsetry or harness systems for richness.

## Mobility equipment and seated construction

For wheelchair, orthotic, prosthetic or other assistive-equipment contexts:

- pattern and seam placement must respect pressure/contact zones;
- closures must remain reachable where source-backed use requires it;
- seat/back contact does not allow cloth to float;
- seams must not be relocated purely to beautify the seated silhouette;
- extra straps or openings may not be invented to make equipment integration visually easier;
- body, clothing and equipment remain one mechanical scene.

## Motion construction

Movement changes load, not topology.

Reaching, twisting, running, crouching and sitting may:

- rotate panels;
- shear cloth;
- redistribute ease;
- open folds;
- tension closures;
- compress material;

They may not:

- move seams;
- add gussets retroactively;
- create new vents or slits;
- change closure type;
- move pockets;
- change sleeve construction;
- change garment inventory.

## Material rule

Construction must be compatible with material behavior.

Do not give:

- heavy rigid material tiny unsupported gathers;
- fragile material extreme load without reinforcement;
- thick material impossibly flat multi-layer intersections;
- non-stretch woven material unlimited body-conforming stretch;
- soft knit crisp armor-like panel boundaries without authority;
- leather/rigid material paper-thin hems or impossible sharp folds.

Material class is stronger than generic anime fold convention.

## Maintenance and repair

Repair must attach to the actual garment construction.

- seam repair follows a plausible seam or tear path;
- patch placement follows damage/use authority;
- repair thread is not decorative rarity trim;
- a repaired closure remains the same closure family unless replacement is authorized;
- generated repair accidents do not become canon.

## Premium rule

Premium does not mean more tailoring complexity.

Premium rendering may improve clarity of already-authorized construction. It may not add:

- extra panel lines;
- decorative topstitching;
- corsetry;
- harnesses;
- buckles;
- chains;
- lacing;
- extra zippers;
- ornamental closures;
- extra vents/slits;
- contrast seam piping;
- body-accent darts;
- gold seam edges.

Detail density authority remains in force.

## LOD / chibi / sprite rule

Small-scale conversion reduces information in this order:

1. tiny stitch detail;
2. secondary topstitching;
3. minor fold branching;
4. hidden facing/lining cues;
5. micro reinforcement detail;

Preserve before those details:

- garment silhouette;
- major panel boundaries;
- closure location/type;
- pocket location;
- layer order;
- major seam logic;
- strap/anchor logic;
- fit family;
- body relationship.

LOD may not simplify every garment into the same tunic/dress/jacket topology.

## Production review checklist

Before accepting a candidate, verify:

- the same garment can be mentally unfolded into one coherent set of pieces across views;
- every visible seam joins or shapes something real;
- every closure has an opening, receiving side, termination and load path;
- pockets have plausible volume and do not migrate;
- straps/belts/buckles have anchors and do not reshape the authorized body;
- seated/crouched/motion states redistribute cloth without changing topology;
- exposure was not increased to solve construction;
- material and seam behavior agree;
- mobility-equipment contact is mechanically possible;
- premium detail has not increased construction complexity;
- LOD removed micro-detail before major topology;
- any unsupported completion remains `CANDIDATE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

## Canon boundary

Generated pattern pieces, seams, darts, pleats, gussets, pockets, closures, straps, reinforcement, lining, facing, repair or hidden construction never become Canon merely because they appeared repeatedly or looked plausible.

Human-reviewed authority is required for promotion.
